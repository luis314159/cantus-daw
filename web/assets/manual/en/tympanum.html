Tympanum, the acoustic drummer

Cantus 0.1.0-beta.15 · Leer en español · back to the manual

Tympanum is Cantus's acoustic drum kit: a kit standing in its room, heard through its microphones, each microphone on a strip of the mixer if you want it there. It plays two kinds of kit. A modelled kit is computed as you play — nothing to download, and its virtual microphones can be moved. A recorded kit is a free multi-microphone sample pack (DRSKit, CrocellKit, Virtuosity Drums, …) or one you have, played in the room it was recorded in. Pulsus stays Cantus's pad drum machine; Tympanum is the drummer.

Add it from the device picker (the + above the track headers, its Drums shelf): Tympanum stands beside Pulsus. A new Tympanum plays the modelled Studio Maple kit with its generated voice — instant, nothing to download.

The Kit view

The window opens on Kit: the kits at the left, the kit in its room in the middle, the pieces and their cards below.

The kits list — All · Recorded · Modelled at its top filters it, and a search box finds a kit by name.

  • Modelled — instant · no download: Studio Maple (rock and pop, twelve pieces and twelve microphones), Jazz Birch (measured off Virtuosity Drums, public domain) and the other modelled kits. A click plays the kit.
  • Recorded — real drums · free packs. A kit not on your disk shows what it takes (Get and its size); a click downloads it from its official source, with its progress and a click to cancel. Some come from the author's site (one click, straight from the author's own page — Cantus never hosts them) and some are Manual: Open the page, download it there (a login, a torrent), then Install from a file…. A kit on disk says On disk.
  • Your own kits — Install from a file… takes an SFZ, a DrumGizmo kit (its .xml, or the archive it came in) or a folder of WAV files. A DrumGizmo kit is built for Tympanum in the background, one file per microphone per hit: the window keeps answering, its box shows the progress (Building its mics · 30 %), and a click on it cancels and leaves nothing behind. The kit you were playing keeps playing until the new one is in.
  • Kits on disk · Manage… — what they weigh; Manage… opens the Content window to update or remove them.

Changing the kit is one undo step and carries your mixer: an output keeps its strip, level and route when the new kit has a microphone of the same name or the same family (Kick, Snare, Toms, Overheads, Room…); a strip made for one microphone is renamed after the new one; strips left with nothing go. A preset that names no kit keeps the kit and its mixer.

The picture — the kit drawn in its room (Drawn or Photographic: Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Instrument pictures). A press on a piece plays it and selects it; Alt-click walks through its articulations; dragging up and down while you press sets how hard. Show mics draws the microphones (on a modelled kit you drag them to move them).

The room card — Room is the picture and the sound in one step: Stage plays the kit's own room (a recorded kit as recorded; a modelled kit dry, on its own), Booth, Studio and Hall put one of Cantus's rooms on the room microphones. Floor, Walls and Rug change the picture only.

Below the picture:

  • the row of pieces with their keys;
  • Feel (the whole kit) — Timing (each hit up to that many milliseconds late, never early), Strength (how much each hit's strength varies from the written one) and Never the same hit twice;
  • Hi-hat pedal — its Openness controller (controller 4 by default): open and close the hat with a continuous pedal; Foot chick when it closes plays the foot's chick;
  • Kit (every piece) — Tune, Ring and Level;
  • the selected piece's panel — its size, Hear (hold it for a roll), its Articulations (each on its key, a click to hear it), Where it is struck (a click on the head sets it and plays there; the edge is brighter), Spread, Sound, Heard by (which microphones hear it) and Open the mixer ›.

Voice: Generated · Hybrid (a modelled kit, under the lit kit in the list). Generated is Cantus's model alone. Hybrid plays, under the model, the first moments of a real kit's hits — each piece's attack as it was recorded — through the same virtual microphones, bleed and room. Its recorded attacks are a free pack of about 5 MB (the jazz kit's from Virtuosity Drums, CC0; the studio kit's from DRSKit, CC BY 4.0): Get · 5 MB once, then it plays offline. Until the pack is on disk, Hybrid sounds exactly like Generated.

Playing it

Tympanum follows the General MIDI drum map (kick on 36, snare on 38, closed hat 42, open hat 46, crash 49, ride 51, …). A few notes past the map play a drummer's figures on the snare: flam on 91, drag 92, buzz 93 (eight quick, soft bounces) and a held roll on 94 (strokes every 55 ms for as long as the note is held); the hat's foot splash on 95. A recorded kit plays its pack's own recordings of its articulations. Every microphone of a hit plays the same take, and a take never repeats the one before it on that key.

The Mixer view

Mixer at the top of the window shows the kit's microphones:

  • The band of microphones (a modelled kit): the selected microphone's Pattern (cardioid, super, hyper, omni), Distance, Aim and Bleed; What it hears — each piece in that microphone against its own; Every mic — each piece in each microphone in dB, a click on a cell turns it off (a recorded kit's microphones stay where they were recorded).
  • Outputs — All to Main (every microphone on Tympanum's own strip), Pieces to strips (one strip per family — kick, snare, toms, cymbals, overheads, room — its microphones into it) and Each mic to its own strip (a strip for every microphone, in order, playing into Tympanum's strip). One undo step each; at unity the split kit sounds exactly like the unsplit one.
  • The strips — each microphone with its meter, polarity (ø), pan, Delay (up to 50 ms to line a far microphone up with a close one; drag it sideways or double-click and type; 0 is as recorded), mute, solo, fader and Out: Main, its own strip or any strip of the desk. A delay changed while the kit plays glides to its new time.

In Cantus's own mixer the kit's strips sit under one band (Drums · Tympanum · 12 mics) that folds into one strip; Each mic to its own strip groups them by family inside it, and a drum bus fed only by them can join the band — see Bands on the desk in the mixer.

Long samples stream from disk through Cantus's sample cache, and a kit loads only the microphones that are on.

What it costs

A modelled kit is computed as it plays: on the laptop Cantus is measured on, the model alone takes about a tenth of one processor core in a busy groove, and every microphone on its own strip about a fifth. A recorded kit costs what its voices cost to stream. Nothing runs while the kit is silent.
