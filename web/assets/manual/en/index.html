Cantus — quick-start manual

For Cantus 0.1.0-beta.1 on Windows · Leer en español

Cantus is a free, open-source digital audio workstation: record, compose, mix and master a song, from the first note to a finished file. This manual gets you there in an evening. Everything it names is in the program as it is today; where the beta still has gaps, chapter 14 says so.

1. Install

You need Windows 11, 64-bit (Windows 10 should work but has not been tested), and about 200 MB of disk.

  1. Download Cantus-0.1.0-beta.1-windows-x64-setup.exe from the releases page.
  2. Run it. It installs for you alone, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Cantus, and asks for no administrator rights. This beta is not code-signed yet, so Windows may show "Windows protected your PC": choose More info ▸ Run anyway.
  3. Choose the components: the program (required), Open .cantus projects with Cantus (on), a desktop shortcut (off), the CLAP and VST3 plug-ins for other DAWs (on), and FlexASIO driver (downloaded now) (off — tick it if your sound card has no ASIO driver of its own; see chapter 2).

Portable copy. Unzip Cantus-0.1.0-beta.1-windows-x64-portable.zip anywhere (a USB stick works). Because of the portable.txt beside cantus.exe, that copy keeps its settings in the data folder next to it and writes nothing to your profile.

Uninstall from Windows' Apps. Your settings, presets and autosaves are removed only if you tick that box; your projects are never touched.

2. First run: set up your audio

The first time Cantus starts, the window Set up your audio opens over the music.

  1. Choose your interface. Each audio device has a card: an Audio interface, This computer, Headphones… Click the one you play and listen through. Cantus picks a driver and a buffer for it: ASIO at 128 samples for an interface with its own ASIO driver, otherwise WASAPI (Windows' own audio) at 256.
  2. Listen. Click Test tone: you should hear a soft tone on outputs 1–2.
  3. Play or speak — the meter should move. Strum, sing or speak into what is plugged in; the input that moves is the one you will record from.
  4. Latency. Cantus says how long the sound takes from string to speaker, and whether that is tight enough to play through. For the most accurate takes, cable output 1 to input 1 and click Measure latency; Cantus then places every take to the sample.

Click Record on … and Cantus adds an audio track on that input, armed and ready: press R to record. Skip closes the window; it does not open by itself again (the Setup assistant… button in the audio chip brings it back).

The audio chip

The chip at the bottom right always shows the device, driver, rate, buffer and latency — for example UMC204HD · ASIO · 48 k · 128 · 6.1 ms. Its lamp is green when the audio runs, amber after a dropout, red when the device is lost or the driver stopped. Click it to change anything; every change applies at once and is remembered for that interface:

  • Interface, Driver (WASAPI or ASIO, with the ASIO driver list), Input device, Rate;
  • Buffer · round trip: 64 to 2048 samples, each with its latency. Smaller is tighter for playing; larger is safer for big mixes. If the status bar counts dropouts, take the next size up;
  • problems appear on top with a button that fixes them (Restart audio, Use this computer's audio, Run at 48 kHz…);
  • Test tone, Channels & routing… (name your inputs and outputs, choose a headphone cue pair), Setup assistant…, and the gear for every setting.

Which driver? Use your interface's own ASIO driver if it has one (Behringer, Focusrite, PreSonus, Steinberg, MOTU, RME…). For a laptop's built-in sound, use WASAPI, or install FlexASIO for lower latency. Get ASIO… in the popover opens a window with FlexASIO's download page, ASIO4ALL's page and the makers' driver pages; after installing a driver, click Rescan drivers. Cantus never installs drivers by itself.

Plugging an interface in offers to switch to it; unplugging one offers your computer's audio, and Cantus goes back to it when it returns. If a driver stops responding, Cantus restarts the audio once by itself. You cannot change the device while recording.

3. The workspace

Cantus opens on a song — the second drop of the demo Neon Tide, looping — so the first thing you hear is music. Press Space to play it.

  • The toolbar (top): the logo and the menus (File, Edit, Clip, View, Help), the project's name (with • when it has unsaved changes), the transport (Go to start, Play, Stop, Record and its ▾ options), Loop, Metronome, Keys (the typing keyboard), the tempo (drag it, wheel it, double-click for 120), the clock, undo and redo, and six window keys: Browser, Arrangement, Channel rack, Piano roll, Mixer, Inspector.
  • The arrangement (centre): tracks and their clips on a timeline.
  • The lower zone: the Mixer, the Piano roll and the Channel rack as tabs.
  • The inspector (right, I): everything about the selected track, strip or clip.
  • The status bar (bottom): what is under the pointer, CPU and dropouts, latency, MIDI inputs, minimised devices, and the audio chip.

Every area can be shown or hidden with its window key or its function key (F5 arrangement, F6 channel rack, F7 piano roll, F9 mixer, Alt+F8 browser, I inspector). Drag the splitters to resize; right-click a tab to float it in its own window; save the arrangement of the window under View ▸ Layout ▸ Save layout…. The interface zooms from 75 % to 200 % (Ctrl+Plus, Ctrl+Minus, Ctrl+0). Colours, themes (light and dark) and the logo are under View ▸ Appearance.

Demo songs. File ▸ Demo songs… (Ctrl+Shift+N) opens six songs made entirely with Cantus's own instruments and effects: Neon Tide (house), Morning Light (pop ballad), Sunday Strut (funk and soul), Paper Lanterns (lo-fi hip-hop), Aurora (cinematic) and Midnight Static (trap). Open one, play it, take it apart.

4. Record audio and MIDI

An audio track

  1. Add an audio track: Ctrl+Shift+A, or right-click the + above the track headers ▸ Add audio track. (The first-run setup's Record on … makes one for you.)
  2. Arm it: click the round red lamp on its header (or Alt+R). Shift+click arms that track alone. An armed track shows its input meter.
  3. Choose its input: the input chip on the header (In 1, In 1-2…; shown when the track row is tall enough), the inspector's Recording section, or the track's menu.
  4. Monitoring: the speaker key on the header cycles Off, Auto (you hear the input while the track is armed — the default) and On. The inspector also has Trim (−24 to +24 dB before recording).
  5. Press R (or the record key). Every armed track records; press R or Space to stop. The whole pass is one undo step.

Takes are written as 32-bit float WAV files: into the project's Audio folder once it is saved, until then into %LOCALAPPDATA%\Cantus\recordings\; saving moves them into the project. Each take is placed earlier by the measured latency, so it lines up with what you heard.

Recording options

The ▾ beside the record key (or right-click it) opens Recording options:

  • Count-in: off, 1, 2 or 4 bars before the take (Alt+C). The count-in clicks whether or not the Metronome (Ctrl+M) is on.
  • Punch: record only inside a range. From selection takes the time selection; Alt+P uses the selection, else the loop, else two bars from the playhead. The range shows as a red brace under the ruler: drag its ends. Everything played is kept in the file; punch only sets the clip's window.
  • Loop takes: with the loop on, each pass becomes a take.
  • Take mode: Stack (old clips move to take lanes — the default), Replace (cut what the take covers) or Overdub (MIDI: add notes to the clip).
  • Click: when the click plays (Always, Recording, Count-in) and its level.

Takes and comping

Every take is kept on a take lane under the track: open them with the small disclosure at the bottom of the track's colour cell, Alt+T, or the track menu's Show takes. Drag across a take to comp that stretch into the track; click a take to use all of it. Right-click a lane for Use this take, Delete unused takes (which also drops recordings nothing plays any more), Explode takes to new tracks and Hide takes.

MIDI

Arm an instrument track (one with no audio input) and press R: notes from every MIDI keyboard Cantus found (the status bar says MIDI inputs: n) are recorded into a clip. With nothing armed, R records into the selected track. No keyboard? Ctrl+T turns the typing keyboard on: A–L play notes, Z/X change octave. While it is on, letter shortcuts (including R) are off — use Space or the record key.

5. Edit: clips, piano roll, automation

Clips in the arrangement

  • Create: double-click an empty place on a track (a 4-bar clip), or record.
  • Select: click; Ctrl+click adds; drag on empty space for a marquee; Esc clears.
  • Move: drag (also to another track). Alt+drag copies; Ctrl while dragging ignores the grid. Left/Right nudge by the grid, Shift by a bar.
  • Trim: drag a clip's edge — its content stays where it is. Slip: Shift+drag slides the content inside the clip.
  • Split at the playhead: Ctrl+E. Glue: Ctrl+J. Duplicate: Ctrl+D (press again to keep tiling). Mute: 0. Rename: F2. Colour: Ctrl+Shift+C.
  • Time selection: drag in the ruler's lower lane; the top strip is the loop. Shift+L loops the selection, Z zooms to it.
  • Audio clips have handles for fade in, fade out and gain (double-click one to reset it; right-click a fade for its curve) and a menu with Normalize, Reverse, Reset clip gain and Remove fades.
  • Import audio: drag WAV, AIFF, FLAC or Ogg files onto the arrangement (they land at the pointer), or File ▸ Import audio…. A MIDI file adds its tracks.

The grid (the arrangement's toolbar) starts at one bar; choose finer values for detail. Scroll with the wheel, Shift+wheel for time, Ctrl+wheel to zoom.

The piano roll

Select a MIDI clip and show the piano roll (F7, or its tab under the arrangement): it edits that clip's notes.

ToolKeyWhat it does

Up/Down transpose the selection (Shift: an octave), Left/Right nudge it, Q quantizes, Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V copy and paste. Velocities are stems in the lane under the grid: drag them, or Alt+wheel over a note. A drum kit shows one named row per pad.

The channel rack (F6) shows every track as a row of steps over the selected clip's bars: click a step on, right-click it off, drag to paint. A step is an ordinary note.

Automation

Right-click any knob, fader or effect control ▸ Create automation clip, or move a control and press Alt+A (automate the last thing you touched). The clip covers the time selection (else the loop, else four bars) on a lane under its track. On the lane: drag points, drag the ring between two points to bend the curve, click the line to add a point, double-click a point to delete it, right-click it for its shape (single or double curve, hold, stairs, pulse, wave). Double-click the clip for the large editor, with a drawing tool and articulators (flip, smooth, random, scale, LFO). An automated control shows a dot.

6. Mix: the mixer, inserts and sends

Each track feeds a strip of the mixer (F9); strips feed the master at the left, or another strip (a bus). A strip has its level meter, mute and solo, pan, fader, phase and swap, width, a three-band EQ, its inserts and its sends. Click to select, Ctrl+click to add, Shift+click for a run; dragging one fader of a selection moves them all. With the mixer focused, M mutes, S solos, Alt+Left/Alt+Right move the strip. Ctrl+wheel changes the strips' width; at Extra large the insert slots show inside each strip.

  • Inserts (up to ten per strip): click an empty slot to choose an effect in the device picker, or right-click it for the quick list. On a filled slot, click the lamp to bypass it and the name to open its window; right-click to swap, move or remove it. In the inspector, each insert has a Mix ring (dry/wet).
  • Sends: select a strip, then look at the Sends row of the others — click the ▲ of the strip you want to send to (a reverb return, a parallel compressor); a knob then sets the send's level. Sends are post-fader.
  • Routing: right-click a strip ▸ Routing ▸ Output or Send to, or select several strips and Create submix from selected.
  • Device windows have Swap, minimise (to the tray in the status bar), maximise and close. F8 opens the selected track's instrument, Esc closes the device you used last, F12 closes every floating one.

Solo follows the routing: a soloed strip is heard with what feeds it and what it feeds.

7. Master: the master chain, Fervor, Latitudo, Culmen

The master has its own chain of up to ten inserts, run on the whole mix before the master fader. Click the master's head to see its chain in the inspector (The whole mix → the inserts → Audio output), or right-click its name ▸ Effects ▸ + Add effect.

The demos master in this order, and it is a good start:

  1. Aequalis (EQ): a high-pass under the sub, a little air.
  2. Multiplex or Comprimo: gentle glue.
  3. Latitudo (stereo): the preset Master: tighten the low end or Master: wider top, mono bass keeps the bass in mono and the top wide. Watch its correlation meter: it should stay above zero.
  4. Culmen (maximizer): choose a target — Streaming −14 (−14 LUFS, −1 dBTP), Apple −16, CD −9, Club −7, Broadcast −23 — then play the loudest part of the song and click Learn drive: Culmen listens, sets the drive, listens again and stops on the target, in one undo step. The Loudness card shows momentary, short-term and integrated loudness and the true peak; the verdict says On target or how many LU you are over or under. Culmen works in three bands plus the full band, into a true-peak brickwall at the ceiling; its Character (Clean, Punch, Loud, Bus, Safe) and its ten presets cover most masters.

Fervor (saturation) adds colour: Tape, Tube, Overdrive, Fuzz, Clip, Fold or a drawn Curve, with oversampling, tone, mix and Level match, so that louder never fools you. On the master, the presets Master clipper and Tube glue on the mid are the gentle ones.

8. Export

File ▸ Export audio… (Ctrl+Shift+E in the default keys) opens the export window: presets on the left, the choices on the right, and a line that says exactly what will be written.

  • Presets: Streaming (MP3 320 kbps, 44.1 kHz, −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP), CD (WAV 16-bit, 44.1 kHz, shaped dither), Stems for mastering (every strip, WAV 24-bit at the session's rate, not normalised) and Podcast (mono MP3 128 kbps, −16 LUFS, −1 dBTP). Change any choice and the Custom card lights.
  • What: the song (from the start to the end of the last clip, plus a tail you choose), the time selection, or stems — one file per mixer strip (after its fader) or per track (before its strip), all starting on the song's first beat, delay compensation included, optionally through the master chain.
  • Formats: WAV 16-bit, 24-bit or 32-bit float; FLAC 16 or 24-bit; MP3 CBR 128–320 kbps or VBR V0/V2/V4/V6 (LAME), gapless; Ogg Vorbis quality 3–10. Sample rate: the session's or 44.1, 48, 88.2, 96, 192 kHz (MP3 uses 44.1 or 48). Mono or stereo.
  • Dither (off, TPDF, shaped) when writing 16 or 24 bits.
  • Loudness: off, or a target (−9, −11, −14, −16, −19, −23 LUFS) with a true-peak ceiling (−0.1, −0.3, −1, −2 dBTP); Culmen's own limiter holds the ceiling when the gain needs it. Stems are never normalised.
  • Tags: title, artist, album, year, comment, and a Cantus encoder credit.

Exports run in the background with a progress bar; Stop leaves no partial file. Your last choices are remembered.

  • MIDI: File ▸ Export MIDI… — the whole song, the selected track or the selected clip, as a type 1 file with the tempo, metre and track names.
  • DAWproject: File ▸ Export DAWproject… to move a project to Bitwig, Studio One and other DAWs that read it (a report lists anything that could not travel).

9. Sounds: the browser and sound packs

The browser (Alt+F8, the first window key) docks at the left. It lists the sound packs you installed and My folders (click + to add any folder of samples).

  • Click a sound to hear it; click again to stop. Type in the search field to find sounds across every pack and folder.
  • Drag a sound onto a Pulsus pad (the pad plays it), onto Exemplar's key map (a zone on that key), onto the channel rack (a new sample channel with its own strip), or onto the arrangement (an audio clip where you drop it). Each drop is one undo step.
  • With nothing installed, the browser offers Start with a drum kit: Download fetches Cantus Starter Drums (81 one-shots, 33 MB). Sound packs… (or More packs…) lists the others — Karoryfer's kits, VCSL and VSCO 2 percussion, a TX81Z for Exemplar — with their size, licence and credits; Install, Update, Remove, Show the folder, and Install from a file… for computers without internet.

Every pack is CC0: yours to use in any song, free, with no credit needed. Packs are saved in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Cantus\Content\; every file is checked against its SHA-256.

10. Instruments and effects: the device picker

The device picker shows every instrument and effect — Cantus's own and the CLAP and VST3 plug-ins installed on your computer — as tiles.

Open it with the + above the track headers (a new instrument track), by clicking a track's instrument chip (swap the instrument), Swap on any device window, an empty insert slot, or View ▸ Device picker…. Search by name, maker or kind; filter by shelf (Favourites, Recent, a category, or Cantus / CLAP / VST3); arrows move, Enter chooses, Ctrl+D marks a favourite, Esc closes. Swapping an instrument keeps its notes, clips, mixer strip and effects; swapping an effect keeps its slot, mix and bypass.

The effects are Aequalis (EQ), Comprimo (compressor), Porta (gate and expander), Sibilus (de-esser), Multiplex (multiband dynamics), Terminus (limiter), Culmen (maximizer), Spatium (reverb), Fervor (saturation), Latitudo (stereo), Delay and Reverb.

Your plug-ins. Cantus scans the standard folders — %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Common\CLAP and …\VST3, C:\Program Files\Common Files\CLAP and …\VST3 — and any folder you add in Settings ▸ Plug-ins (Scan for plug-ins, Rescan everything). Each file is scanned in a separate process, so a plug-in that crashes while being scanned is blocked, not fatal. Cantus's own effects are installed in your CLAP and VST3 folders too, for other DAWs.

11. Projects, autosave and settings

  • File ▸ New project (Ctrl+N), Open… (Ctrl+O), Save (Ctrl+S), Save as… (Ctrl+Shift+S). A project is a .cantus file; saving keeps the previous one as .cantus.bak.
  • Audio files are collected when you save: recordings move into the project's Audio folder and imported files are copied there, so the folder travels whole. Collect audio and save does it on demand. A missing file shows MISSING on its clip; right-click it ▸ Locate file….
  • Autosave writes a recovery copy every minute when something changed (Settings ▸ Saving, 10 s to 10 min): <project>.cantus.autosave beside a saved project, or in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Cantus\autosave\ for an unsaved one. It never overwrites your file. Writing it never freezes the window (it runs in the background).
  • Recover your work: if Cantus closes unexpectedly — or you close it without saving — the next start offers what was left: the autosaved changes and any recorded takes that are in no saved file. Restore brings them back as one undo step (Ctrl+Z shows the last save); Discard deletes nothing. New and Open ask before replacing unsaved work.

Settings (View ▸ Settings…): General (the verse at start, on or off, and the Spanish Bible), Audio (driver, devices, rate, buffer, latency and the record offset), MIDI, Appearance (language — English or Español —, interface size, colours, keyboard shortcuts), Saving, Updates and Plug-ins.

13. Privacy and updates

Cantus has no telemetry. An installed or portable copy asks GitHub once a day whether a newer version is out — an anonymous request that carries no version, machine or user information — and asks you before downloading anything; Settings ▸ Updates ▸ Check daily turns it off. Sound packs are downloaded only when you click Install. Everything else stays on your computer. The details are in PRIVACY.md; the licences of everything Cantus is built from are in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md (also in Help ▸ About Cantus).

14. Known issues and limitations

This is a beta. What we know about:

  • A plug-in made by someone else that crashes closes Cantus (plug-ins run inside the program; isolating them comes later). Save often.
  • MP3 files cannot be imported (WAV, AIFF, FLAC and Ogg can).
  • No time-stretch: audio clips keep their speed when the tempo changes; one tempo and one time signature per project. No crossfades or clip gain envelopes.
  • Hosted plug-ins are not rendered into stems, and their parameters cannot be automated yet; automation is drawn, not recorded (no latch or touch).
  • Lamina and Exemplar are 6–8 dB quieter than the other instruments at the same fader.
  • Audio: the built-in Realtek ASIO driver delivers no sound on some laptops (use WASAPI or FlexASIO); ASIO4ALL can take several seconds to start; many ASIO drivers set the buffer in their own control panel. Exclusive-mode WASAPI is not offered.
  • Performance: dense projects at small buffers can drop out on modest computers; raise the buffer if the status bar counts dropouts.
  • The count-in is silent unless the metronome is on; the audio device cannot be changed while recording; the sample browser has no keyboard navigation yet; projects refer to sound-pack samples by their full path.
  • Not in this beta: macOS and Linux, warping, MIDI learn and control surfaces, AI features, several arrangements per project.

15. Report a bug

Help ▸ Report a bug… opens the bug form on GitHub (you need a free GitHub account). Say what you did, what you expected and what happened; give your Cantus version (Help ▸ About Cantus), Windows version, audio interface, driver, rate and buffer (all in the audio chip); attach a small project that shows the problem if you can. Tell us about your audio interface even when it works, with the hardware report, and send ideas with Help ▸ Suggest a feature…. English or Spanish, both are welcome.
