What an AI agent can do in Cantus

Back to the manual · Leer en español

When you let an assistant such as Claude connect to Cantus (Settings ▸ AI), it works on your project through tools — the same edits you make with the mouse, each one a single step of Undo named "AI: …" in the history. Reading never asks; changing asks in the Cantus window unless you allowed edits; removing or replacing your material always asks; files are refused until you allow them, and then asked. The names of your tracks, clips and markers are shown to the agent as data — never as instructions.

An agent never takes your material silently: drawing automation over points you drew, a part where your notes are, a preset over your settings all ask first (or are refused).

Positions are given in beats (quarter notes from the start) or as bar:beat (9:1, 9:2.5), and Cantus answers with both. Values are in each parameter's own unit (dB, Hz, ms, semitones…); a value outside its range is refused with the range, never quietly changed.

The agent also has the server's own tools (ADR 0081): it can wait for or cancel a long job, group a turn of its work into one undo step (begin_change/end_change), undo its own last step and list what it did.

The catalogue of units

list_units and get_unit (and the resource cantus://units) describe every instrument and effect Cantus has: each parameter's key, its name, its group (Band 4), unit, range, default and taper, the names of a switch's choices, the factory presets and what they set, the starting preset an effect loads on insert, its latency, and what the unit is for. It is made from the same tables the knobs use, so it is always complete and always right.

The music linter

lint_music reads the notes the way a reviewer listens, and says where and why:

  • a note ringing into the next chord that does not hold it — an error when it is a semitone against the new chord (the classic: a chord's A♭ still sounding over E♭/G's G);
  • a clash: a note held under a chord a semitone above one of its tones (the 11th over a major chord's third, the ♭6 over the fifth) — a 9th, a 13th or a major 7th is a colour;
  • a doubled note: the same pitch written twice at once on a track;
  • outside the key and the chord (info: often a passing note, sometimes a mistake);
  • outside the instrument: below a piano's A0, a drum note with no pad.

Give it the chords (the chart) for the harmony checks; without them it checks doubles, the key and the range. It changes nothing.

Charts: chords in bars

The writers and the linter read chords the way a lead sheet writes them, one bar between bars |, the chords of a bar sharing it equally:

text Fm9 | Dbmaj7 | Abadd9 | Eb Eb/G C . . G7 | % C for three beats, G7 for one; then the bar again

. holds the chord before for one more share, % repeats the bar, N.C. is no chord. Any symbol works: C, Cm, C7, Cmaj7 (CM7, CΔ), Cm7b5 (Cø), Cdim7, Csus4, C6/9, Cadd9, C9, C13, C7b9, C7#11, C7alt, slash basses (Eb/G).

write_part lays a part over a chart — block chords, pulses, arpeggios or a bass line, on a rate or a step rhythm (x..x..x.), with swing and a human touch — voiced to move as little as possible from chord to chord, and no note rings into the next chord. voice_chords writes the held chords; pedal_from_chart draws Chorda's sustain pedal: up on each change, down again a moment later, so every chord rings and never the last one's.

Prompts

A client lists ready-made requests: Explain my mix (changes nothing), Write a part, Arrange an intro, Tidy the project (asks before removing anything) and Teach me this unit, in the language of your interface.
