Chorda: the modelled piano, recorded pianos and the piano's mixer

Cantus 0.1.0-beta.15 · Leer en español · back to the manual

Chorda is Cantus's piano. Its own sound is modelled: strings, hammers and soundboard computed as you play, nothing to download. It can also play a recorded piano — a free sample pack (the Salamander Grand, bitKlavier's Steinway D, Upright Piano KW, …) or one you bought — with the same window, the same notes and the same mixer strips. This chapter is about choosing the source, getting the pianos, mixing their microphones and the two things a recorded piano adds: Add our room and a microphone's delay.

Choose the source

Open Chorda (double-click its track). The Source list is at the left of the Play view; All · Recorded · Modelled at its top filters it.

  • Modelled — Chorda 2, today's model. Previous models unfolds to Chorda 1, kept so older songs sound as they did.
  • Recorded — the free pianos Cantus knows, the Salamander Grand first. A piano not on your disk shows what it takes (Get · 742 MB); a click downloads it from its official source, with its progress and Cancel. A piano on disk says On disk; the one playing says Playing. Piano in 162 and Helsinki 1967 Pro come only from their authors' pages (a torrent, a login): Open page, download it there, then Install from a file….
  • Your own pianos — what you installed from a file.

A click on a piano that is on disk switches the source: one undo step. Your notes, your automation, your strips and the mixer's routes stay; switching back to the model plays its settings exactly as they were.

Install from a file…

The dashed box at the end of the list takes an SFZ, a Decent Sampler preset, an SF2 or a zip / tar.gz of one. A download of a piano Cantus knows (recognised by its size and checksum, whatever its name) installs as that piano; anything else becomes one of Your own pianos, played where it is (an archive is unpacked into Cantus's content folder). Tympanum's kits list has the same box for drum kits.

The Play view

As the design draws it: the piano in its room (the picker of the piano and its finish over the picture; the room, floor, walls and rug on the card over its corner — the look, never the sound), the keyboard under it, the Hammer, Resonance and Output cards, and The piano at the right:

(The picture is drawn Drawn* — light, the default — or Photographic, with traced reflections and soft shadows: Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Instrument pictures. The sound never changes.)*

  • Lid — closed, short stick, full stick.
  • Perspective · Where you listen — from over the hammers to the hall; the mics of the mixer it reaches are listed under it, and Open the mixer › goes there.
  • Pedals — Sustain and Soft show where the player's foot is (controllers 64 and 67) or where you set them; Dampers sets how firmly the dampers stop the strings; the curve under shows the dampers against the pedal, the half-pedal band shaded.

Moved in this version: Chorda's Room level is the Mixer's Room strip fader, the Width is the Mixer's Piano strip (Chorda 1 keeps it in Output), Dampers is the row under Pedals, and the macros are in the Edit view. The first time you open the new Play view, the status line says so.

The Mixer view

Mixer at the top of the window shows the piano's own mixer:

  • The picture and a card for the selected output: its name as the pack names it, who recorded it and its licence. For bitKlavier, Images on disk — how many of its nine microphone images you have, what they take, and Get the other n.
  • Outputs — All to Main puts every output back on the piano's own strip; Each mic to its own strip gives every output a strip of its own in Cantus's mixer, in order, playing into the piano's strip. One undo step each. At unity the split piano sounds exactly like the unsplit one.
  • The strips — Piano at the left (the whole piano's level; the model's Width), the outputs under their family heads (Perspectives, Noises; a head folds its strips), and Room at the right. Each output has polarity (ø), pan, mute, solo, its fader and its Out cell: Main, its own strip, or any strip of the desk.
  • bitKlavier's images. Each microphone image is its own download (about 1.5 GB on disk each). An image not on disk is a dashed strip with its download; an image on disk loads or unloads from its top cell (as recorded · unloaded): an unloaded image takes no memory.
  • The model's five outputs (Hammers, Player, Audience, Room, Mechanics) are listed; until the model renders them apart they play together on Main, and the row says so.

Add our room

A recorded piano carries the hall it was recorded in (its far microphones hear it). A piano recorded close or dry may want more room: on the Room strip, Add our room steps through Off · Small room · Studio · Hall — the rooms Tympanum's kits play in. When it is on, the strip's fader is the room's level; it plays on the piano's own strip. Off adds nothing: no room is built, no memory taken, the piano sounds exactly as before.

A microphone's delay

Each output has a delay (up to 50 ms) to line its microphone up with another — a far pair arriving later than the close one — or to push it later on purpose. It is the Delay cell on every output strip, under ø and pan: drag it sideways (0.05 ms a point, Shift 0.01), or double-click it and type a value (Enter keeps it, Esc leaves it); 0 is as recorded and adds nothing. Each change is one undo step, and it can be drawn as automation. A delay that changes while the microphone sounds glides to its new time (no click, no silence); at rest the output is delayed by exactly the whole samples you set. Cantus builds a delay line only for the outputs that have a delay or an automation lane on it; the first time an output gets one, the notes sounding at that moment stop.

Disk and memory

  • Packs are kept as FLAC when they are installed: bitKlavier's images take about 1.5 GB each instead of 3.8 GB; every file is checked sample for sample before the original goes.
  • Long samples stream from disk through Cantus's sample cache (Sample cache… in the settings). Several bitKlavier images at once fill that cache fast (about 4 GB each); raise its limit if you play four or more together.
  • One image costs about 6 % of one processor core; four images about 25 %.
