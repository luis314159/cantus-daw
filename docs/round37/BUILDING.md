## Briefs

### piano — Chorda by ear (ADR 0098, schema 54), then Sectio (ADR 0099, schema 55)

Read: ADR 0059, `docs/instruments/chorda.md`, BACKLOG §3e item 4 and its two round-28 notes
(*Chorda must sound better*), `research/instruments-next/notes.md` §6, ADR 0023 (the family's
rules), ADR 0042 (CC BY content).

**Chorda — the sound first.** Its measurements pass and the owner's ear says no. Work by
comparison, not by physics alone:
1. **References.** Install the Salamander Grand through Cantus's own pack (CC BY 3.0; download
   into `downloads\piano\`, never the repository) and render the same phrases through it and
   through Chorda: single notes at pp/mf/ff across the range (A0…C8 every minor third), soft
   chords, a fast run, a pedalled ballad, a ff chord, bass notes alone, the top octave. Write
   a comparison tool (`cantus-ui/examples/chorda_compare.rs` or in `cantus-bench`) that
   loudness-matches and reports, note by note and band by band: the spectral envelope over the
   first 50 ms and at 0.5 s, the attack transient (the hammer's thump and noise), the decay
   envelope per partial band, the stereo image, the release and damper noise. Plots as PNG.
2. **Close the gaps** in a **model 2** (Chorda's `model` key): the hammer's felt and noise, the
   soundboard's body and radiation (a woody resonance, not a filter), brightness across
   velocity, the release and damper, the bass's weight, the treble without a metallic edge,
   the strings' stereo spread, and **a player/close perspective with a room** (convolution
   from an impulse we generate or measure, or an algorithmic room — no third-party IR unless
   CC0). Model 1 stays bit-identical for every old song: **schema 54's migration writes
   `model = 1` explicitly into every existing Chorda spec**, a new instance gets model 2, the
   golden hashes of model 1 do not move (`DSP_VERSION` stays 5 unless a golden moves on
   purpose). The family's tests hold for model 2 (null at rest, block-size independence at 0,
   offline = real time, parallel = serial, 64 pedal-down voices within 25 % of a core at
   256 frames).
3. **The owner's ABX kit** (`cargo run --release -p cantus-ui --example chorda_abx -- <dir>`):
   renders loudness-matched pairs (Chorda 2 vs the Salamander, Chorda 1 vs Chorda 2) of the
   phrases above to WAV, and writes **one offline HTML page** (no network, no external
   scripts) that runs a proper ABX — A, B and X per trial, X drawn at random, at least 16
   trials, answers stored locally, the score and the binomial p-value at the end — plus a
   preference question per phrase. The kit's README (EN and ES) says how to take it.
   `docs/beta-checklist.md` gets the steps for the owner. Measured results go in your report;
   **only the owner's ABX closes Chorda**.
4. Chorda's presets redone by ear for model 2 (at least 16, tagged by style), its page and
   manual updated, shots in both themes. Report to the integrator, with the numbers, before
   Sectio.

**Sectio — the slicer** (BACKLOG §3e item 1; FL's Slicex and Fruity Slicer 2, Ableton Simpler's
slice mode, Logic Quick Sampler, Bitwig 6.1 Sliced). A mode of Exemplar: drop a loop, it is
sliced. Embedded markers read first (WAV `cue `/`smpl`, ACID `acid` chunk — test with files
from outside sources: a published ACIDized WAV or one written by another program, with its
licence), then transients (`onset.rs`, SuperFlux for sustained material, one sensitivity
control), a beat grid (`beat.rs`), or manual slices; slice points **stored in the project**
(schema 55); one slice per key from C1; per-slice pitch, gain, pan, envelope, filter,
reverse, choke group; **Dump to piano roll** (the loop replayed from the dumped notes equals
the file to the bit at the file's tempo); a tempo change keeps slices on the grid (slice
replay); FL's *Random*, *Stutter*, *Quantize/Swing* as one undo step each; drag a loop onto
an empty channel or the browser's *Slice* → a sliced Exemplar in at most two clicks. **Done
when** ≥ 95 % of slice points are within 10 ms of hand annotation on a set of CC0 loops
(download them; record each loop's source and licence), the dump replays bit-exact, the
e2e test drops a loop through the app and plays the dumped clip. Agents: `slice_sample`,
`dump_slices`. Manual EN/ES, shots both themes.

### drums — Pulsus III (ADR 0100, schema 56)

Read: BACKLOG §3e item 3 and item 11's *Still open*, ADR 0023, ADR 0038 (voices across the
pool), ADR 0022 (what is heard), ADR 0064 (freeze), ADR 0092 (the recording mode — a split kit
is several strips), ADR 0093 (VCA), the pads model and `pulsus.rs`.

1. **Instruments with several outputs.** Extend the engine's instrument contract so a unit can
   render named stereo buses (Pulsus first; Exemplar's DRSKit microphones are the next user —
   design for it, do not build it unless it is small). Each pad has an output: *Kit* (the
   track's strip) or another bus; a bus feeds a strip. **Split to strips** in one click
   (Pulsus's menu and the mixer): one strip per used pad, named after the pad, with undo as
   one step and *Merge back*. **Null first:** a split kit with every new strip at unity sums
   to the unsplit kit to the bit; block-size independence at 0; offline = real time;
   parallel = serial; freeze, the recording mode, solo/mute, VCAs, sends, delay compensation
   and render/export all see the new strips correctly (a test each); `rt_memory` counts
   nothing on the path.
2. **A chain per pad**: insert effects on a pad's voices before the kit's sum (any Cantus
   effect, hosted plug-ins if the insert mechanism allows it cheaply — decide in the ADR),
   shown on the pad; **chains at rest cost nothing** (measured).
3. **Kits from outside**: a folder dropped onto Pulsus (or the browser's *Make a kit*) becomes
   a mapped kit — names parsed (kick, snare, clap, hat open/closed, toms, crash, ride, perc;
   EN and ES words; numbered variants as velocity layers or round robins), the rest by order;
   **SFZ drum kits** imported (test with a real CC0 SFZ kit from outside — e.g. one of the
   packs we already list — and its licence). A kit drop is one undo step.
4. **Original kits**: 808- and 909-class kits (and one or two more styles: trap, lo-fi, house)
   **synthesised by Pulsus itself** or rendered from Cantus's own instruments, ours, CC0 —
   never sampled from a machine or a pack. Tagged so library's browser finds them.
5. **Done when** 16 pads, each with its own chain, split to 16 strips in one click; the split
   kit sums to the unsplit one to the bit; a folder becomes a mapped kit in one drop; an e2e
   test does all three through `CantusApp::assemble` and the real engine. Agents:
   `split_kit_outputs`, `set_pad_chain`, `make_kit_from_folder`. Manual EN/ES, shots both
   themes (the pads with their chains, the split mixer).

### library — presets, packs, the website (ADR 0101, schema 57 only if it must)

Read: BACKLOG §5, §3b (*effects sound on insert*, option B), ADRs 0030, 0042, 0050, the
preset code above, `web/src/units*.rs`, `render_fx_ab.rs`, the owner's
`feedback-showcase-sell` (the site and README must *sell* the instruments and effects).

1. **An audit first**, as a test: every instrument kind and every effect kind has at least
   **N factory presets** (decide N in the ADR from the leaders — FL and Ableton ship dozens per
   device; instruments ≥ 24, effects ≥ 10 is the floor to beat), each tagged by **style** from
   the styles most produced today (trap and hip-hop, pop, house/EDM, R&B, lo-fi, rock, latin
   and reggaeton, ambient/cinematic, film/game) and by role (bass, lead, pad, keys, pluck, FX,
   drums; for effects: vocal, drums, bus, master, creative). The test fails on a kind below N.
2. **Presets that are worth hearing**, made with care: each instrument preset rendered by a
   test on a fixed phrase per role — no NaN, no silence, no clipping, loudness inside a window
   so switching presets does not jump (±2 LU of the role's target), CPU under the kind's
   budget; effects on fixed sources (drums, vocal, bus) the same way. Then judged by listening
   where you can (describe what you listened for); the owner's ears are the last word.
3. **Instrument gain staging** (§5): Lamina and Exemplar sit 8–9 LU under the others at unity —
   calibrate every kind to one reference level; old projects keep their sound by a migration
   (schema 57) that writes a compensating gain where it changed.
4. **Finding and hearing presets**: the preset menu and the browser filter by style and role,
   search by name, and **audition** a preset on a phrase (instruments) or on the selected
   clip (effects) without changing the project until chosen (one undo step when applied).
   Factory presets in a data format if the arrays no longer scale (decide in the ADR).
5. **Cantus's own packs on demand**: one-shots and loops rendered from Cantus's instruments and
   the presets (ours, CC0), and a vetted list of more CC0/CC-BY packs where they fill a style
   gap (each licence checked at its source, recorded). Our own packs are hosted as release
   assets of `luis314159/cantus-daw` under a `content-…` tag with SHA-256s pinned in the
   manifest — **prepare the files and the manifest; the integrator uploads them** with the
   owner's permission at the end. The manifest tests (licence, credit, hash) hold.
6. **Heard from the website**: every instrument and effect card on the landing page plays
   short excerpts of its presets (rendered by the program, loudness-matched, compressed — keep
   the site's size in check and say the total), honest labels (*rendered by Cantus, no
   processing after*); README's unit grid links them. Shots both themes, EN and ES.
7. **Done when** the audit passes for every kind, the e2e test opens the browser through the
   app, auditions and applies a preset as one undo step, and the site plays every unit.
   Agents: `list_presets` gains style/role filters; `audition_preset`, `apply_preset` (or
   extend the existing ones — check `agent_tools.rs`). Manual EN/ES.
